Privacy policy: the KidHome apps

Made by Oddvalue. Effective 24 October 2026.

This policy covers these apps:

  • KidHome
  • KidHome Listen
  • KidHome Sunrise
  • KidHome Routine
  • KidHome Music
  • KidHome Books
  • KidHome Games
  • KidHome Scribble
  • KidHome Letters
  • KidHome Balls (called 99 Balls on the phone)
  • KidHome Print
  • KidHome Stop Motion

In short

The apps don't collect, share or sell any information about you or your child. None of them has permission to use the internet, so they can't send anything off the phone. There are no ads, no accounts, no analytics, no crash reports and no in-app purchases. The coins in Games and 99 Balls are earned by playing and can't be bought.

Everything the apps keep is stored on the phone or tablet they're installed on. The rest of this page says what each app keeps, where it keeps it, and why it needs each permission.

How every app works

Your parent PIN

The apps keep parent settings behind a four-digit PIN. KidHome stores the PIN as a salted hash, so the PIN itself is never saved. The other apps ask KidHome on the phone whether a PIN is right and get back only yes or no. If KidHome isn't installed, an app keeps its own PIN the same way, as a salted hash on the phone. 99 Balls and Print have no parent settings and no PIN.

Backup

The apps turn off Android's backup to Google Drive and its copying of app data to a new phone. Settings, PINs, recordings and drawings inside an app stay on the phone they were made on.

Files saved where you can see them

Some apps save what your child makes as ordinary files, so you can find them in the phone's Files or Gallery app:

ListenAudiobooks/Recordings/, or a folder you choose

The apps never send these files anywhere. Other apps on the phone can see them, as with any photo or recording. If you use a backup or sync app such as Google Photos or Syncthing and turn it on for one of these folders, that app copies the files under its own privacy policy. Google Photos doesn't back these folders up unless you turn them on.

Pictures and files picked with Android's picker

When an app opens Android's own photo or file picker, it can open only the items picked. The apps that use it are Scribble (a photo to draw on), Print (pictures to print) and Stop Motion (a sound track).

Language

Each app remembers the language chosen for it.

What each app keeps and reads

KidHome

  • The home screen you build: page names, which apps are on it, tile colours, labels and emoji, and your child's first name if you type one in for the greeting.
  • Your parent PIN, as a salted hash, and whether locked folders have been unlocked today.
  • Whether the app guard is switched on.
  • KidHome reads the list of apps installed on the phone so you can put them on the home screen.

App guard. If you turn on the app guard, KidHome uses Android's usage access to check which app is on screen while the screen is on, so it can bring your child back to KidHome and ask for your PIN. It reads this on the device as it happens and keeps no record of which apps were used. It also asks Sunrise whether a night is in progress, so it can keep Sunrise in front.

Google Family Link. KidHome reads which apps Android has hidden or paused, which is how it follows the limits you set in Family Link. From the parent screen, behind your PIN, it can open the Family Link app or website, or KidHome's page on Google Play, in your browser. It doesn't read your Family Link account, settings or activity. KidHome isn't made by or affiliated with Google.

KidHome Listen

  • Listen reads the music and audiobook files in Music/ and Audiobooks/, or in the folders you choose, so it can show and play them. It never uploads or changes them.
  • It saves where each audiobook was left, the folders you chose, and your last sleep timer and sleep sound choices.
  • It makes the sleep sounds (rain, ocean and so on) on the device.
  • Recordings. On the Record tab your child can record their voice with the microphone. Listen saves each recording as an audio file in Audiobooks/Recordings/, or a folder you choose, and plays it back like a story. Holding a recording's cover and confirming deletes that file. The microphone is used only while recording.

KidHome Sunrise

  • Wake-up and nap times for each day, brightness, the timer, and whether a night is in progress.
  • Any events you add, such as "Grandma's visit", with their date and emoji.
  • Sunrise reads what Listen is playing and its sleep timer, so it can show them at bedtime. It tells KidHome when a night is in progress.

KidHome Routine

  • The routines you set up, with their steps and pictures.
  • Which stickers your child has earned on which days.

KidHome Music

  • Tunes your child saves: the notes played, plus a name and emoji. Music remembers notes, not sound, and doesn't use the microphone.

KidHome Books

  • Books reads the pictures and comic book files in Pictures/Books/, Pictures/Family/ and Pictures/Scribble/, or in the folders you choose, so it can show them as books. It doesn't change them.
  • It remembers the last page read in each book.
  • Books your child makes in the Make tab, with the titles and words they type. Their pages are saved as pictures in Pictures/Books/.
  • It can hand a picture to Scribble on the phone for drawing on. Scribble works on a copy, so the original stays as it was.

KidHome Games

  • Games reads the photos in Pictures/Family/, or a folder you choose, to make jigsaws and pairs games. It doesn't change them.
  • Coins earned and what they've unlocked.

KidHome Scribble

  • Your child's drawings, inside the app, with a copy of any photo they draw on.
  • A picture of each saved drawing in Pictures/Scribble/, or a folder you choose.
  • Scribble opens a photo only when your child picks one with Android's photo picker, or when another app on the phone, such as Books, hands one over to draw on.

KidHome Letters

  • Your own voice saying letters and numbers, if you record them in the parent settings. The recordings stay inside the app. The microphone is used only while you record.
  • For any letter you haven't recorded, Letters asks the phone's text-to-speech voice to say it. That voice is part of Android, not Letters. Letters has no internet access, but some text-to-speech engines can use the internet. You can choose the engine in Android's settings.

KidHome Balls (99 Balls)

  • Coins earned and what they've unlocked. 99 Balls asks for no permissions.

KidHome Print

  • Print uses Bluetooth to find your label printer and send it the pictures to print.
  • It remembers the last printer's Bluetooth address and your print settings (darkness, brightness, contrast, gap).
  • It prints pictures picked with Android's photo picker, pictures shared to it from other apps, and documents sent from Android's print menu. A document is copied to a temporary file while it prints and then deleted. Print keeps nothing it printed.
  • Location on Android 11 and older. Those versions of Android only let an app scan for Bluetooth devices if it holds the location permission. Print asks for it on those phones for that reason only. It never reads your location. On Android 12 and later, Print uses Android's Bluetooth scanning permission marked as not for location, and doesn't ask for location.

KidHome Stop Motion

  • Stop Motion uses the camera to take the frames of your child's film. Frames can show whatever the camera points at, including faces.
  • It uses the microphone to record a narration, if your child adds one, and can use a sound track picked with Android's file picker.
  • Frames, narration and sound tracks stay inside the app until your child exports a film. Exported films are saved as videos in Movies/StopMotion/.

Children

The apps are made for children to use after a parent or carer sets them up. Children can't send us any information through the apps, because the apps have no way to send anything.

Removing your data

Uninstalling an app, or clearing its storage in Android's settings, removes everything it keeps inside the app. Files saved to the folders listed above stay until you delete them in the phone's Files or Gallery app. Your own music, audiobooks and photos aren't touched.
